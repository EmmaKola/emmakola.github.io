Project Overview

NovaMed Solutions is a pharmaceutical distributor operating across Europe and other international markets. This project analyses one year of sales data—including revenue, profit, product performance, and customer demographics—to improve visibility across commercial and operational performance.

The analysis was designed to identify revenue drivers, assess product and customer performance, highlight market opportunities, and translate the findings into practical recommendations for planning and decision-making.

About the Business

NovaMed Solutions supplies pharmaceutical products to hospitals, smaller distributors, resellers, and direct users. Its commercial performance depends on reliable product availability, effective inventory planning, and a clear understanding of customer and market demand.

A consistent analytics view of sales, cost, profit, product, customer, and geographic performance can help the business prioritise resources and strengthen evidence-led planning.

Business Problems

NovaMed Solutions was not using its sales and customer data to its full potential. This limited visibility into overall performance and made it more difficult to identify product, customer, and geographic opportunities.

The analysis addressed four decision areas:

  • Sales performance visibility: Limited tracking of revenue, profit, cost of goods sold, and month-over-month movement.
  • Product performance: Difficulty identifying high- and low-performing products consistently enough to support pricing, promotion, and portfolio decisions.
  • Customer insight: Limited understanding of customer demographics, purchasing behaviour, and buyer segments.
  • Geographic opportunity: Insufficient visibility into regional sales performance and underdeveloped markets.

Project Objectives

The project aimed to turn NovaMed's sales and customer data into a reliable performance view for commercial and operational decision-making.

The core objectives were to:

  • Identify revenue-driving customer segments: Determine which groups contribute most to sales performance.
  • Analyse profit margins: Compare profitability across products and geographic markets.
  • Evaluate sales patterns: Identify trends that can inform forecasting and revenue planning.
  • Understand customer demographics: Profile high-value customer groups and buying behaviour.
  • Develop an interactive dashboard: Build a drill-down Power BI experience with filters and slicers for self-service analysis.
  • Prepare a stakeholder-ready report: Present findings and prioritised recommendations for action.

Data Analysis Process

The workflow followed a structured approach to improve data quality, analytical reliability, and reporting clarity:

  1. Data collection: Sourced and validated raw data for completeness and accuracy.
  2. Data cleaning: Removed duplicates, corrected inconsistencies, and handled missing values.
  3. ETL: Transformed, structured, and loaded the prepared data into Power BI.
  4. Data modelling: Established table relationships and created a model to support consistent analysis.
  5. Calculations: Created DAX measures, KPIs, and calculated fields.
  6. Dashboard development: Designed interactive visuals, slicers, and drill-down features for decision support.

Executive Summary

The final dashboard brings revenue, profit, product, customer, and geographic measures into one decision-ready view. It highlights product pricing questions, customer concentration, reseller dependency, and markets that warrant further investigation.

Key Insights

Revenue and profit fluctuated across the year rather than following a consistent growth path. January, July, and September recorded clear peaks, while February declined sharply. The repeated peaks may reflect demand cycles, but additional operational and market data would be required to confirm seasonality. Profit moved closely with revenue and maintained a broadly consistent gap, suggesting relatively stable cost behaviour during the period.

Doxycycline, Ergocalciferol, and Lisinopril form the strongest revenue group, while Clonazepam and Ezetimibe follow closely. The relatively even contribution across these products reduces dependence on a single top-selling medication.

Together, the five products provide a broad revenue base at the top of the portfolio.

The percentage contribution view confirms a balanced mix among the top five products, with no single medication dominating revenue.

Hydrochlorothiazide, Fluticasone, Amoxicillin, Montelukast, and Warfarin generated substantially less revenue than the leading products. Pricing, unit volume, procurement cost, competition, and demand should be examined before attributing the gap to a specific cause.

While the top‑earning medications drive NovaMed’s financial strength, the quantity‑sold analysis reveals a more nuanced picture of market behaviour.

Doxycycline ranked first for revenue but second for units sold, with unit sales approximately 36% below Lisinopril. Lisinopril therefore warrants a price-and-cost review to understand why stronger volume does not translate into the highest revenue. Hydrochlorothiazide, Montelukast, and Amoxicillin also recorded relatively high unit demand but lower revenue, indicating a need to assess pricing and product economics.

Aspirin and Omeprazole recorded the highest profit-margin ratio at 0.98, followed closely by Escitalopram. Doxycycline, the highest-revenue product, recorded a lower ratio of 0.95. The difference warrants a closer review of pricing, procurement cost, discounts, and product mix.

Doxycycline shows a consistent gap between revenue and profit, which supports a review of product-level cost efficiency. Sales peaks appear early, mid-year, and near year-end. Revenue and profit rise and fall together, showing that profitability is closely linked to changes in sales volume or value.

Aspirin shows a more stable and closely aligned revenue-and-profit pattern. The limited divergence indicates a consistent profit-to-revenue relationship and relatively stable product economics during the reporting period.

Male customers contributed the most revenue across the reporting period, peaking at $3.3M in May. Female customers and the Other category followed a similar pattern at lower levels, indicating broadly comparable movement with different revenue contributions.

Preferred Customers—hospitals and retailers—contributed 36% of total revenue, two percentage points above New Customers at 34%. Frequent Buyers contributed the remaining 30%. The relatively balanced split suggests that retention and acquisition both matter to revenue performance.

Male customers contributed the largest share within each segment, while the Other gender category showed a pronounced decline among Frequent Buyers.

Resellers generated 88% of total revenue, 76 percentage points more than direct users. This concentration makes reseller retention commercially important while also exposing the business to channel-dependency risk.

Revenue was distributed relatively evenly across age groups, suggesting broad demographic reach rather than dependence on a single age band.

Canada generated the highest revenue at $32M, while the United States generated the lowest at $6M. Australia contributed $15M—approximately half of Canada's result. Other markets each contributed about 8%–9%, highlighting regions that require further demand and market-potential analysis.

A country-by-segment drill-down shows that Preferred Customers drive revenue in the highest-performing market. Contribution varies across other countries, as shown in the dashboard.

Recommendations

  • Review product economics: assess pricing, procurement cost, and discounts for Lisinopril, Hydrochlorothiazide, Montelukast, and Amoxicillin, where unit demand is not translating proportionately into revenue.
  • Protect efficient products: monitor Aspirin's 0.98 profit-margin ratio and test targeted demand initiatives without weakening its cost performance.
  • Develop segment strategies: retain high-value male customers while testing evidence-led campaigns for lower-contributing segments, including the Other gender category.
  • Manage reseller concentration: protect the channel generating 88% of revenue through pricing, service, and partnership measures, while evaluating direct-user growth opportunities to reduce dependency risk.
